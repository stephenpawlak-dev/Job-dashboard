📖 How these numbers work (methodology)

1. Job Match Fit: 35% of Overall Fit Score

Business/Technical/Operational Mix, Stakeholder Management, Ambiguity to Execution, Influence vs. Formal Team Leadership (scores high when a role rewards influence-without-authority, a real strength this profile has, not a workaround for a gap), and Technology Fit (Cloud Networking / Cloud Security / Agentic AI and Ops).

2. Qualification Fit: 30% of Overall Fit Score

Added to separate soft fit signals from the actual qualifications a posting lists. Every role's Minimum Qualifications are checked against a canonical set of qualifying facts (20 years at Cisco, 9+ years GTM strategy and product management since 2017, 10+ years sales experience, an MBA plus a BS in Computer Science, and no formal people management but extensive cross-functional leadership through influence). A minimum requirement that is genuinely ambiguous against those facts still gets an investigate flag on the card, it does not disqualify a role, it just marks something worth confirming before applying. Only a requirement specifically about managing people, managing people-leaders, or owning a budget can trigger a flag on leadership grounds, generic led-teams or cross-functional language never does.

Every qualification, minimum and preferred, is scored 1 to 5 for real fit and blended into one number: minimum qualifications carry a flat weight of 1 each, preferred qualifications carry a flat weight of 1.25 each, a 25 percent premium per item since preferred quals differentiate a candidate rather than just clearing the bar. Weighted average, scaled by 20. Qualifications are no longer separately weighted by perceived importance within their own category, each counts equally there, minimum vs. preferred is the only distinction that carries different weight. This intentionally keeps a handful of weak preferred items from overriding several strongly cleared minimum requirements, since minimum requirements are the real bar and there are usually more of them. Roles without a formal scraped posting (HPE, Meter) have no minimum qualifications to weigh, so their score runs entirely off preferred-style items built from the hiring manager's actual stated bar, clearly flagged as such on the card rather than left unscored or backed into a fabricated posting.

3. Career Trajectory: 25% of Overall Fit Score

Intrapreneurial Latitude, Growing Segment/BU, CRO/CTO Path Match, Cross-Business Breadth, BD/Metrics Ownership.

4. Likelihood to Accept: 10% of Overall Fit Score

50% Culture, itself an average of five factors: Work/Life Balance, Travel Load, Location Mode (Remote scores best, Hybrid better, In-Office still good), Hiring Manager Relationship (this one is explicitly provisional, since it updates as real HR/HM interviews happen), and Overall Culture (growth opportunity, hire-from-within pattern, stability of the team/role).

50% Comp, using total comp (cash plus equity) checked against your three personal bands and shown only as a verdict: Not Meeting, Meeting, or Exceeding. No dollar figures are displayed anywhere on this page.

Overall Fit Score

Overall Fit = 0.35 × Job Match Fit + 0.30 × Qualification Fit + 0.25 × Career Trajectory + 0.10 × Likelihood to Accept

Likelihood to Accept dropped from 20% to 10% weight. It measures whether Stephen would want the role, culture and comp, not whether the employer would extend one, so it should not carry equal weight against the three factors that actually predict getting an offer in the first place. It still counts, comp and culture are real, just not as a co-equal driver of overall fit.

Close Confidence Score

Close Confidence = 0.20 × Job Match Fit + 0.70 × Stage Confidence + 0.10 × Likelihood to Accept

Stage dominates deliberately. Fit only matters once you are actually through the early gates. A perfect-fit role sitting at Applied still will not score high here.

Curve updated again based on further research into how these funnels typically convert. Stage Confidence is the baseline for a deal's actual formal stage, with one exception: a real, highlighted hiring manager personally engaged ahead of Second Stage gets a 25 percent increase applied to that stage's baseline, a proportional bump, not a flat point add, so it cannot leapfrog real formal progress later in the funnel, see the outlier callout below. That boost stops applying at Second Stage and beyond, real formal progress already covers it there. Separately, personal and hiring manager connection strength that is not this specific pre Second Stage boost is still real and useful, it gets you in the door, and it lives in Likelihood to Accept, specifically the Hiring Manager Relationship sub-factor. A genuine, direct relationship with the actual hiring manager scores a 5 there, a warm personal contact who is not the hiring manager holds at a neutral 3.

🔎 Outlier: Meter. Tyler Crowley, the SE hiring manager, reached out unprompted, ahead of Second Stage, so this deal's Hiring Manager Discussion baseline (25%) gets a 25 percent increase, landing Stage Confidence at 31%, still below HPE's real Second Stage baseline of 40%. An early, warm conversation should not outrank genuine formal progress, it is a real signal, just not that big a one. HPE no longer needs the boost, it has genuinely progressed to a formal Second Stage interview loop, which is past the point where this override applies.

Master metric: confidence of landing something in 30 days

This is the number at the top of the page, and it changed shape after review. The first version combined all 5 opportunities as if they were fully independent bets. That math is real, but it produces a high number even when every individual deal is weak, purely because there are 5 of them running at once. It felt wrong because it was overstating things: these processes are not fully independent in reality, since market conditions and your overall positioning affect all of them somewhat together.

The version after that was deliberately dominated by the single strongest opportunity, full credit for the top deal, everything else pooled and heavily discounted. That undercounted a real situation: once more than one process is genuinely past a hiring manager conversation, they are each a real, separate shot, not noise to be averaged into a backup pool.

Current version, a stage-cohort model. Only deals that have reached at least Hiring Manager Discussion count toward this number at all, earlier-stage roles are excluded entirely, not discounted, excluded. Applied is real progress and still shown on that role's own card, but it does not move this specific number until a hiring manager is actually engaged. Every counted deal is combined using its own Close Confidence, standard probability of landing at least one of several bets: 1 minus the product of each one's chance of not closing. More roles at this stage raises the combined number on its own, that is intentional, more real shots in flight should mean more overall confidence.
